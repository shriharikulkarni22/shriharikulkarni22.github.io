Chart 1 — Ambient temperature sweep

Chart Conclusion

T_j rises linearly with ambient — the slope equals R_θ(total), so a worse heatsink or fewer vias steepens both curves equally.

The vertical gap between the two curves is constant across the entire ambient sweep. It equals P × R_θ(mask) — the mask penalty is a fixed temperature offset regardless of ambient.

Design takeaway: If either curve crosses the 150°C dashed limit within your operating ambient range, the design is unsafe at that condition. Removing the mask (Case 2) shifts the curve down by a fixed amount but does not reduce the slope — a better heatsink or more vias is still needed to flatten the rise.

Chart 2 — Thermal via count sweep

Chart Conclusion

Via count has strongly diminishing returns. Doubling vias from 1→2 halves R_θ(via); doubling from 8→16 adds far less benefit because the total resistance is now dominated by R_θ(j-c) and the heatsink.

The gap between Case 1 and Case 2 is constant at all via counts — confirming that mask removal benefit is independent of via density. You gain the same ΔT_j whether you have 2 or 20 vias.

Design takeaway: Beyond roughly 8–12 vias (at this pad size), additional vias yield negligible T_j improvement. Invest instead in heatsink quality or mask removal to make a meaningful difference.

Chart 3 — Heatsink Rθ sweep

Chart Conclusion

Heatsink Rθ is the dominant variable. Unlike via count which saturates quickly, T_j keeps rising steeply as heatsink resistance increases — because R_θ(HS) sits in series with no parallel path.

The absolute gap between curves widens slightly as heatsink resistance rises, because a higher total R_θ amplifies the fractional contribution of the mask layer. Mask removal is proportionally more valuable with a poor heatsink.

Design takeaway: Never undersize the heatsink hoping mask removal or extra vias will compensate — they cannot. Heatsink selection sets the T_j ceiling; mask and via optimisation fine-tune within that ceiling.

Variables, Formulas & How to Use

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your MOSFET's R_ds(on) from the datasheet and the expected drain current. Power dissipation P = I²·R is computed automatically.
  2. Enter your board's pad size, PCB thickness, and via drill diameter. These set the geometric thermal resistance of the via array.
  3. Enter R_θ(j-c) from your MOSFET datasheet and the nominal ambient temperature for your application (85°C for automotive under-hood, 70°C for ADAS, 25°C for benchtop).
  4. Click Apply & Recalculate. Metric cards and all three charts update immediately.
  5. Use the sliders inside each chart to sweep a second variable (heatsink, via count, or ambient) while holding the others fixed.
  6. Compare the coral (Case 1, mask ON) vs teal (Case 2, mask OFF) curves. The vertical gap between them at any X point equals the junction temperature rise caused solely by solder mask under the pad.
  7. The dashed 150°C limit line marks T_jmax. Any curve crossing it signals a design violation at those conditions.

Two Cases Modelled

Case 1 — Mask ON (conservative)

Solder mask remains over the thermal pad. Heat path: Junction → Package case → Via array (FR4 substrate) → Solder mask layer → TIM (poor, k = 0.5 W/m·K) → Heatsink.

Case 2 — Mask OFF (optimised)

Mask is opened on the thermal pad. Heat path: Junction → Package case → Via array → TIM (good Cu-loaded, k = 4.0 W/m·K) → Heatsink. R_mask term is eliminated.

Governing Equations

Conduction power loss
P = I² × R_ds(on)
Via array thermal resistance (parallel copper columns)
R_θ(via) = t_PCB / (k_Cu × n_via × π/4 × d_via²)
Solder mask thermal resistance (Fourier slab)
R_θ(mask) = t_mask / (k_mask × A_pad)
TIM thermal resistance
R_θ(TIM) = t_TIM / (k_TIM × A_pad)
k_TIM = 0.50 W/m·K (Case 1, mask ON) | k_TIM = 4.0 W/m·K (Case 2, mask OFF)
Total thermal resistance — Case 1 (mask ON)
R_θ(total,1) = R_θ(j-c) + R_θ(via) + R_θ(mask) + R_θ(TIM,poor) + R_θ(HS)
Total thermal resistance — Case 2 (mask OFF)
R_θ(total,2) = R_θ(j-c) + R_θ(via) + R_θ(TIM,good) + R_θ(HS)
Junction temperature estimate
T_j = T_amb + P × R_θ(total)
Design constraint
T_j < T_jmax = 150°C  (AEC-Q101 Grade 1)

Model Assumptions & Limitations

• All thermal resistances are modelled as one-dimensional, steady-state Fourier slabs in series. Lateral spreading resistance is not included.

• The via model assumes vias are copper-filled solid cylinders. Hollow vias or via-in-pad with partial fill will have higher R_θ(via) than shown.

• R_ds(on) is treated as constant. In practice it rises ~0.2–0.6%/°C, so actual losses are higher at elevated T_j (conservative correction: multiply by 1.3–1.5 for high-temperature operation).

• Switching losses, gate driver dissipation, and body-diode conduction are not modelled.

• k_mask = 0.20 W/m·K and TIM conductivities are fixed literature values. Verify with your actual materials.

• Heatsink R_θ is treated as lumped, ambient is uniform, and natural convection enhancement from the PCB is neglected.
